# The Þing — how a meeting is run

**What it is.** the house meeting framework: four roles, decisions by consent with one
Approver, grades A/B/C, and a sign-off that makes the record official.

## The four roles

- **Chair** — runs the meeting, names each decision in one sentence, closes it, signs the
  record last.
- **Secretary** — owns the record: publishes within 24 hours, runs the sign-off, appends
  corrections.
- **Notetaker** — Snotra records the transcript; nobody writes by hand.
- **Adjuster** — challenges the decision, names the option nobody named, records dissent.
  The Chair is never the Adjuster.

## Decisions

One Approver per decision, named before discussion. Consent, not unanimity: a proposal
passes unless there is a reasoned objection. Every decision carries a grade:

- **A** one-way/strategic — every participant signs explicitly; review date mandatory.
- **B** costly to reverse — Approver and affected owners sign.
- **C** easily reversed — silence for two business days is acceptance.

## Sign-off

Reply `confirm` or `objection: <what is wrong>`. Signing confirms the **record is
accurate**, not that you agree with the decision. States: Draft → Reviewed → Approved →
Signed. Corrections are appended, never rewritten. The Chair signs last.

## Cadence

Daily written check-in · Monday 45 min (the week) · monthly 90 min (trajectory) ·
quarterly half day (strategy, roles, the A decisions). A question from a higher layer is
parked, not decided, in a lower one.

The generic framework and templates ship with the `snotra-meeting-outcomes` skill under `assets/meeting-framework.example.md` and `assets/templates/`; a house copies and adopts them.
