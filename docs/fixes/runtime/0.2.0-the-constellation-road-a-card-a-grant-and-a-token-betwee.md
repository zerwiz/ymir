## runtime · 0.2.0 · 2026-11-03 — The constellation road: a card, a grant and a token between two fleets

### Why
Plan 172. Two independent houses could not talk without one handing the other a seat in its own suite; now they exchange a card, a skill-scoped grant signed by both keys, and a five-minute token.

### Files
- `bin/bridge/constellation.sh`
- `bin/bridge/constellation-tool.mjs`
- `bin/bridge/constellation-grants.py`
- `bin/gates/guards/constellation-grant-guard.sh`
- `bin/bridge/tests/test_constellation.sh`
- `config/grants.schema.json`
- `src/ymir_runtime/grants.py`
- `bin/gimle/mcp-read.py`

### The fault

There was no way for two independent Ymir fleets to exchange a single question without one of them
handing the other a **seat inside its own house**: an Authelia account in the group `team`, a suite token
scoped `*`, a forge user. That is membership, not federation — and it is why "he can just publish randomly
in our fleet with our access" was a fair worry rather than a hostile one. The discovery half had existed
since plan 61.1 (`.pi/extensions/constellation` reads a registry of cards and refuses to call without a
grant); the middle — the grant and the token — had never been built, so `constellation_ask` refused by
design and nothing could cross.

### What changed

- **`bin/bridge/constellation.sh`** — one door, nine verbs: `keygen · publish · list · show · grant ·
  countersign · activate · mint · verify · revoke · guard · doctor`. TOON output, exit 0/1/2/3, every
  refusal naming what failed.
- **`bin/bridge/constellation-tool.mjs`** — cards, keys and tokens. Cards are validated against the ONE
  shared contract (`packages/contracts/src/agent-card.ts`, imported at run time — no second definition of a
  card) and refused when they carry a secret. Tokens are EdDSA JWTs over the sender's ed25519 key.
- **`bin/bridge/constellation-grants.py`** — writes and reads the grants shelf in the **existing** plan-58
  registry shape, never reimplementing its law.
- **`config/grants.schema.json` + `src/ymir_runtime/grants.py`** — the shape gained `skills[]`, and `state`
  gained **`pending`**: two houses sign at different moments, so a grant signed by one party is `pending`,
  may not be used, and is exempt from the missing-signature refusal *and nothing else*. `activate` is the
  only way to `active`, and it refuses until every party's Heimdall has signed.
- **`bin/gimle/mcp-read.py`** — the token file's optional fourth field is the subject's **fleet**, carried
  into every `access.jsonl` row: a ledger that cannot say which house a read came from cannot answer the one
  question an incident asks.
- **`bin/gates/guards/constellation-grant-guard.sh`** — the ward: a grant may not name a skill the peer's
  card does not declare, an active grant past its expiry is a closed road, and a grant naming a peer with no
  card is refused. A plan-58 namespace grant (no `skills`) is not a road grant and is not judged.

### What was proved

`bin/bridge/tests/test_constellation.sh` — **27 assertions, all passing**, in a throwaway home and
registry: keygen · publish both cards · a card carrying a secret refused · list reports peers · an
unconfigured registry says UNKNOWN (never zero) · a grant for an undeclared skill refused · a grant written
`pending` · a token cannot be minted for a pending grant · countersign verifies the peer's signature over
the identical canonical body and activates · mint · verify for the right audience and skill · refused for a
wrong audience · refused for a wrong skill · refused against another key · an expired token refused ·
revoke · no token after revocation.

The realm law was re-proved separately against `src/ymir_runtime/grants.py`: `pending` with one signature is
accepted, `active` with one signature is refused naming the Heimdall that did not sign, and `active` with
both is accepted.

### What is deliberately not here

The transport call (`constellation_ask` carrying over A2A) and the inbound delivery-by-injection half are
**not** in this change: both need the peer's door to exist before they can be proved, and a fake transport
is the exact lie the discovery half was built to avoid. They remain phases C3/C4 of plan 172, and the
refusal that names the missing grant stays until they are real.

### One neighbour repaired

`bin/gates/guards/defaults-guard.sh` was tracked **mode 100644** (not executable), so `bin/gates/guards.sh`
ran it with `"$ward"` and got `Permission denied` — the whole ward set reported FAIL on a clean tree, and the
one ward that could never run was the one that checks every path resolves through the resolver. Made
executable here; the bad mode came in on a commit that never noticed, which is what a suite that fails for
the wrong reason does to a house.
